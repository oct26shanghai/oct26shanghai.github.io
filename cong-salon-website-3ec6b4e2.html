About

We are living through strange times. How do we make sense of this moment, at once disorienting and delirious, frustratingly boring and excitingly unpredictable, anxious and ecstatic, mundane and sublime?

This salon launches a long-term conversation across Mainland China, Hong Kong, the US, and Europe, bringing together creative practitioners and curious thinkers to articulate the ideas, questions, art, and philosophies that can help us navigate this transformative moment.

The Threads: Provocations

Our conversations are guided by the following thoughts and observations.

Provocation #1
一
posted by
Curatorial Team		We are less interested in who wins the US-China "race" in AI and robotics than in how the planetary technosphere has long emerged from transnational competition and exchanges between regions. We center on what moves or sits in tension between China and the West, historically and now, from tea and porcelain to computers and cellphones. Today's moment is layered in a long history of such encounters. Rather than taking sides, we want to stay within the tension. In between is where everything interesting occurs.
Provocation #2
二
posted by
Curatorial Team		We are deeply committed to aesthetics, which, following Kant, we understand as a special kind of thought grounded in the free play of the imagination. In this, we share a growing recognition that taste, style, and beauty are critical to technological evolution, but we want to deepen and extend these conversations. We are intrigued by how today's "China-maxxing" and Xin Zhongshi fashion trends connect with older layers of Japonisme and Chinoiserie. Deeper still lies the aesthetic revolution of the Song Dynasty and the Chan (Zen) minimalism that sought a beauty outside of time.
Provocation #3
三
posted by
Curatorial Team		We are struck by how accelerating AI is reawakening deep cosmic and theological questions. Religion seems to be returning, or perhaps it never went away. In the US, this ranges from the Pope's reflections on AI to Peter Thiel's talks on the Antichrist. In China, it appears in a renewed interest in Buddhism, Daoism, and especially divination. Are these best understood as reactions to the failure of a rational, secular order? How do they compare? As society moves further into the weird and unknown, what new forms of religion and spiritual practice will emerge?
Provocation #4
四
posted by
Curatorial Team		The term Sinofuturism was coined in the 1990s to describe an orientation toward time. We are curious about how to make sense of it now. From the wave of "TikTok refugees" flocking to Xiaohongshu, to the launch of DeepSeek, to China's vast robotics industry, China increasingly appears as the wave of the future. Yet inside China, the mood is different. People speak of tangping (lying flat), neijuan (involution), and guo rizi (simply getting by). There is also a turn back toward nature, visible in ecovillages, a revival of craft, and a broader analog turn. What is Sinofuturism now?
